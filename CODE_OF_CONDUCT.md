# Code of Conduct

chard is a small project, but the rules are the same ones that work everywhere else.

Like many open source projects, chard depends on contributions, collaboration and the
patience of people who did not write the code they are reading. When people work together
there are cultural, communication and compatibility issues, so this is a brief code of
conduct we ask everyone taking part to follow. It applies to everyone regardless of standing
in the project, and anyone can remind anyone else of it.

No list is ever exhaustive, so we ask you to follow the spirit rather than the letter, as that
is how it will be enforced. It applies to issues and pull requests, and to other direct
interactions about the project. Violations, especially continued or flagrant ones, may affect
your ability to take part.

Much of what chard measures belongs to other people. When reporting a finding to another
project, follow that project's code of conduct, and take the finding to them before publishing
it.

## Rules

- **Be friendly and patient.** We were all new, or short of some piece of knowledge, at some
  point. Try to remember what that felt like.

- **Be welcoming.** We aim to welcome and support people of all backgrounds and identities.
  That includes, and is not limited to, members of any race, ethnicity, culture, national
  origin, colour, immigration status, social and economic class, educational level, sex,
  sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, age, size, family status, political
  belief, religion, and mental and physical ability.

- **Be helpful.** Helping others learn enriches the whole project. Mentor each other where you
  can.

- **Be considerate.** Your work will be used by other people, and you depend on the work of
  others. Decisions you take affect users and colleagues, and this is a worldwide project, so
  you may not be talking to someone in their first language.

- **Be respectful.** We will not all agree all the time, but disagreement is no excuse for poor
  behaviour or poor manners. Frustration is not licence for a personal attack. A project where
  people feel uncomfortable or threatened is not a productive one.

- **Be careful in the words you choose.** Be kind. Do not insult or put down other
  participants. Harassment and exclusionary behaviour are not acceptable. That includes, and is
  not limited to:

  - Violent threats or language directed against another person.
  - Discriminatory jokes and language.
  - Posting sexually explicit or violent material.
  - Posting, or threatening to post, other people's personally identifying information
    ("doxing"), whether or not it is publicly available.
  - Personal insults, especially those using racist, sexist or otherwise discriminatory terms.
  - Deliberately referring to others by names or pronouns counter to their identity.
  - Unwelcome sexual attention.
  - Advocating for, or encouraging, any of the above.
  - Repeated harassment. If someone asks you to stop, stop.

- **When we disagree, try to understand why.** Disagreements happen, technical and social
  alike, and resolving them constructively matters more than winning them.

## Reporting

If you believe someone is in violation of this code, in letter or in spirit, email as detailed
a description as you can of what happened and who was involved to
[security@hexproof.dev](mailto:security@hexproof.dev).

## Consequences

- Except in flagrant or egregious cases, a first infraction results in a warning. Everyone
  slips up; we ask that you not repeat it.
- A second infraction, or a more flagrant first one, may have an anonymised summary of the
  interaction published, as a reminder that the behaviour is not tolerated.
- A third infraction, or an especially egregious first one, results in temporary suspension
  from participation in the project for four weeks.
- Continued infractions are handled case by case, and may result in permanent suspension.
